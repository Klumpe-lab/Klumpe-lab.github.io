Research

What we work on

Transposable elements (TEs) are mobile genetic elements that can replicate and insert themselves into new genomic locations. We study the cell biology that drives their replication cycle, and we develop the cryo-FIB and cryo-ET methods to see it happen inside cells and tissues.

01

Transposons in the germline

TEs are widespread across the tree of life and often make up a large part of their host genomes. In mammals they account for roughly 25–75% of the genome, and in humans about 50%. One subclass, the long terminal repeat (LTR) retrotransposons, moves via an RNA intermediate and is related to retroviruses.

Left unchecked, TEs are a severe threat to genome integrity. Metazoans have evolved intricate transcriptional and post-transcriptional defence mechanisms to protect their germline genomes. We are fascinated by the cell biological mechanisms that drive the replication cycle, and thus the evolutionary success, of TEs:

  • How do retrotransposons reach the germline genome to generate new insertions?
  • What drives the sex-specificity observed for TEs?
  • How do TEs access nuclear genomes in the first place?

To answer these questions, we use model systems such as Drosophila melanogaster to study TEs in their niches during gametogenesis, the generation of sperm and egg. Using cryo-ET, we have previously determined the cellular structural biology of the copia retrotransposon.

02

Seeing molecules where they work through technology development

"Progress in science depends on new techniques, new discoveries, and new ideas, probably in that order." Sydney Brenner

In that spirit, we combine structural biology with technology development in cryo-focused ion beam milling (cryo-FIB) and cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET). Cryo-FIB milling thins frozen cells and tissues so that cryo-ET can image membranes and individual protein complexes in their native environment, in favourable cases at resolutions below 1 nanometre.

Sample preparation from large specimens

We want to make cryo-ET of multicellular organisms and tissues routine. We have established Serial Lift-Out on campus and are exploring shaped ion beams to section material from high-pressure-frozen specimens, now also on the Hydra Bio PlasmaFIB at the Vienna BioCenter.

Open-source software & data

With new funding, we are merging SerialFIB and OpenFIBSEM into a single community platform, fibsemOS. For data analysis we develop CryoBoost, a browser-based pipeline from raw tilt series to a searchable, segmented database of tomograms that we share with the Vienna BioCenter community.

Where we are heading

In the years to come, we will build an integrative research program that combines biochemistry, systems biology and fly genetics with the latest developments in cryo-ET. Our goal is to describe and understand the mechanisms underlying (retro)transposition.

Our expertise in cryo-ET, combined with the outstanding facilities and collaborative spirit of the Vienna BioCenter, puts us in a unique position to uncover the cellular structural biology of LTR retrotransposons, how they are regulated, and their impact on germline integrity.
